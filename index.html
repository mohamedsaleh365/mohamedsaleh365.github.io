Research

Two disciplines, one question.

How can a system act optimally under hard real-time and energy limits? Predictive control answers with models and constraints. Spiking networks answer with sparse, event-driven computation. I work on both, and on what happens when they meet.

Control anchor

Real-time predictive control for unstable robots

End-to-end nonlinear MPC, from physics-based modeling to a solver that closes the loop on embedded hardware.

  • Lagrangian gray-box modeling and multiple-shooting system identification
  • Direct multiple shooting NMPC with soft safety constraints
  • acados Real-Time Iteration with HPIPM, benchmarked on ARM
  • EKF output feedback and DMDc data-driven surrogates
Learning anchor

Energy-efficient spiking networks for multimodal perception

Attention-based fusion that stays linear in cost, so spiking networks can go deep and hierarchical across modalities.

  • Binary cross-modal Query-Key attention (CMQKA)
  • Hierarchical multi-scale audio-visual fusion
  • LIF neurons trained with surrogate-gradient BPTT
  • Neuromorphic deployment pathways (Intel Loihi)
⇄

Where they meet

Integrating predictive control with learned representations: learned and data-driven models inside optimal controllers, and efficient learning that respects real-time budgets. I want to take this toward power systems, robotics, and renewable energy.

Control · RoboticsM.Sc. thesis and extension · 2024–2025

Nonlinear MPC for a two-wheeled self-balancing robot

A complete control stack for an unstable inverted pendulum on wheels, rebuilt end to end in Python: identify the plant, design the controller, make it fast enough for an embedded 200 Hz loop, and close the loop under noisy, partial sensing.

  • 8-state symbolic CasADi model with DC-motor dynamics, identified by multiple-shooting prediction-error optimization to 88–91% tilt-prediction fit on held-out data.
  • Corrected a spurious stable tilt mode that closed-loop identification had introduced in the thesis model.
  • acados RTI with HPIPM reaches a 143× speedup over offline IPOPT (0.88 ms vs. 125 ms) with the same closed-loop behavior.
  • Raspberry Pi 4 benchmark at 200 Hz: mean 2.58 ms, p99 3.12 ms, across horizon, condensing, iteration, and integrator sweeps.
  • EKF output feedback with exact CasADi Jacobians matches full-state feedback for about 9% extra compute.
  • The original M.Sc. thesis deployed constrained linear MPC (QP) from MATLAB/Simulink on the physical robot, with validated Sim2Real transfer.

Machine learning · NeuromorphicPLRI, MHH Hannover · 2025

SNNergy: hierarchical multimodal spiking networks

Attention-based audio-visual fusion models cross-modal structure well but costs O(N²), which rules out deep multi-scale architectures. Cheaper concatenation misses complementary information. SNNergy removes that trade-off.

  • Identified audio-visual learning as an underexplored direction in energy-efficient SNNs and scoped the research problem myself.
  • Designed CMQKA, a bidirectional, symmetric cross-modal Query-Key attention built from binary spike operations, with linear O(N) cost and learnable residual fusion.
  • Built the first hierarchical multi-scale cross-modal fusion framework in spiking computing, over three stages at H/4, H/8, and H/16.
  • State of the art among SNN-based multimodal methods, surpassing several ANN baselines.
  • Proposed an Intel Loihi deployment pathway for real-time edge inference.

Scientific computingComputational electromagnetics · 2026

FDTD for dispersive, time-varying media

A 1D finite-difference time-domain solver for Maxwell's equations in a medium that is both dispersive and modulated in time, in the visible regime. Built in stages, each checked against a closed form before the next feature went in.

  • Three reproducible verification gates: static slab against the Airy sum (R + T − 1 = 5.8e-14), Drude slab against analytic reflectance, and a temporal boundary against temporal Fresnel coefficients (0.3747 vs. 0.375).
  • A pulse crossing a Drude slab with a modulated plasma frequency splits into a sideband comb at ω₀ + nΩ.
  • Sideband energy follows perturbation theory: fitted exponents 1.82, 1.93, 3.86, 3.95 against 2 and 4.
  • Measured second-order convergence (order 2.08) and a time step derived from the plasma CFL limit, so no run is unstable by oversight.
